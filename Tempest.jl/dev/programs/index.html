Programs

A program is Julia array code, and what it computes reaches the compiler as three things: maps, folds (a scan is a fold that keeps the axis it runs along, a scatter one that combines each element at a position it computes), and reads at index formulas. Products, reshapes, permutations, gathers and scatters are spellings of them, and so is every function a program calls.

What Tempest names

Tempest names only what Base cannot spell:

  • the way in: compile, explain, gradient and withgradient, the targets, and the implementations of kernel packages Tempest has extensions for;
  • the derivative of a scalar function, which its code does not carry: scalar_partials, and a value no gradient flows through: stopgrad;
  • the laws of a fold's combine: GenericFold.

Folds and scans

A fold is Base's mapreduce, its state a tuple if need be, and a scan Base's accumulate; a fold's derivative comes from its combine's, through scans.

A function given to mapreduce is taken to associate and commute, as a reduction groups and interleaves its elements; one given to accumulate is combined in sequence, as Base combines it, from an init that need be no identity.

A GenericFold, given to reduce or accumulate where the function goes, claims that it associates and commutes, and so a scan by it may group its elements, as a sum's may.

logsumexp

logsumexp is a fold, a spelling. The log-sum-exp written out with maximum differentiates as it does, and plans as it does where the target permits the rewrites of sums (see Compiler.rewrite); it is named still because where a target keeps the rounding as written, the spelling is two passes, the maximum and then the sum, and logsumexp one.

Other functions

Any other function, a product by labelled axes or a batched product say, is a spelling and lives in its own package, as Einops's and NNlib's do; an extension gives it a method on traced arrays where its body does not trace.
